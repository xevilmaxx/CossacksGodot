`0.299R+0.587G+0.114B`) → `numpy.histogram(L, bins=8, range=(0,256))`, i.e.

### 8.3 Why the criterion conflicts with donor flatness (derived, arithmetic)

For a donor-like unimodal near-planar surface (mean ≈96, std ≤10), an 8-bin
32-level histogram has its mass in bins 2 `[64,96)` and 3 `[96,128)`; the
neighbouring bins lie ≥32 levels from the mean (≥3.2σ), contributing ≪3%.
Placing 4 *contiguous* bins at ≥3% forces the L span to ≥96 levels, which for
a donor-like distribution implies std ≳17 — violating the **same criterion's
own std ≤10** gate. Only contrived rare tails in bins 1 and 4 at their edges
nearest the mean could satisfy both numbers; that would be metric-gaming,
would introduce the speckle/comb defect the visual gate forbids, and would be
*less* donor-faithful than the current 2-bin top. **No genuine render fix
exists that satisfies this sub-threshold while preserving donor plateau
flatness.** I did not attempt one.
